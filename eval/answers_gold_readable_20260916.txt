GOLD chunk(s): ['olympia_brca_os_2022_chunk_0002']   retrieval_hit: True
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The mechanism of synthetic lethality that makes BRCA1/2-mutated cancers sensitive to PARP inhibitors like olaparib involves the inhibition and trapping of the DNA repair enzyme, poly (adenosine diphosphate-ribose) polymerase 1 (PARP1). This occurs because cancers harboring germline pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants in BRCA1 or BRCA2 (gBRCA1/2pv) are characterized by homologous recombination DNA repair deficiency following the inactivation of the wildtype allele during tumor evolution. This deficiency engenders selective sensitivity to inhibition and trapping of PARP1 on DNA, exploiting the concept of synthetic lethality, as functional homologous recombination is required for cell survival when PARP1 function is inhibited and PARP1 is trapped on DNA, arresting the DNA replication apparatus [SOURCE 2].
